Measures and data

Greenery measures. Our on-road measures come in two versions: unweighted counts all roads equally; weighted weights each road by how central it is in the street network (space syntax choice, 800 m radius), so busier roads count more.

Outcomes. NHS prescribing in 2016 for diabetes, hypertension, asthma and opioids, plus all prescribing, each as yearly quantity per registered patient. Prescriptions are shared out from GP practices to areas by where each practice's patients live.

Areas and controls. 632 London wards and, as a check at a finer scale, 4,835 LSOAs. All models control for the share of White residents, median age, deprivation and building density.

Models. Propensity score matching (PSM; areas above vs below the median of a measure), double machine learning (DML; the measure kept continuous, effect per SD) and OLS (standardised coefficients).

Cost estimate

Weighted street view on-road greenery and diabetes prescribing. Change in a year's diabetes prescription items in London, and its cost at London's 2016 cost per item (; NHSBSA English Prescribing Dataset). Negative values are fewer items and lower cost. These numbers hold only if the associations are causal and the same across the range of greenery: a first-order approximation, not a forecast.

  • DML, +1 SD: every area's greenery rises by one SD; its items change by the DML effect per SD.
  • PSM, to median: areas below the median change by the PSM effect (above vs below the median), applied to their items.

Prescribing against diagnoses

Do the prescribing outcomes track how many people are diagnosed? Pearson correlation across the 637 London wards between each outcome and the QOF disease registers of 2016/17 (SI Table S1). Diabetes and hypertension prescribing match diagnoses closely and asthma reasonably well; depression does not, which is why depression and anxiety are left out of the models.
